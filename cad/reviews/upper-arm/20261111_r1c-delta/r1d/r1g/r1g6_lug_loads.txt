HOW THE FORCES WERE INTEGRATED
- Face rows: hub-side nodal stresses, traction t = sigma . n (n = the hub's outward normal), interpolated linearly over each face and
  integrated on a 0.1 mm grid. Z faces over the flat lug face (X offset 18.76-34.4, Y 288-294.8, less the Dia 3.8 hole); outer face
  over Z 1-11, Y 288-294.8; bolt-hole wall over the cylinder. The force is what OUR ARM APPLIES TO THE HUB.
  Centre of pressure = the compressive-pressure-weighted centre on each Z face. "p mean / p max" = the normal pressure over the
  loaded part and its nodal peak (MPa).
- Section rows: the stress integrated over a Y 294.5 cut through each lug (interior nodes, not extrapolated surface values). They
  must cancel the face forces below the cut; the sign is opposite. The difference measures the nodal over-read of the face rows.
- NO over-count correction is applied to the face rows. The -X lug faces close to their section within 5 %; the +X lug faces read
  16-17 % high (the back-face edge peak, 115-133 MPa nodal). USE THE SECTION TOTALS PER LUG; use the face rows for the split between
  faces and the centres of pressure.
- Whole-hub section at Y 310 (above the disc): everything the arm puts into the hub. Balance: applied vs that section. Moments close
  within 2-3 %, Fz within 10 %. Its Fy reads -9 to -41 N where 0 is applied: integration error on the hub's coarse 4 mm mesh at
  Y 310 (bending stresses that should cancel). The "ring, ridge, groove and spigot" share = applied minus the lugs (sections).

THE LUG'S OUTER FACE AND THE SIDEWAYS LOAD (r1g6; corrected 2026-11-13 after the shoulder thread's question)
Sign convention: every face row is the force ON THE HUB, the integral of sigma.n over the hub face with n the hub's outward normal.
For the -X lug, +X is inward (toward the yaw axis). The section row is the force the hub ABOVE Y 294.5 exerts on the lug BELOW it, so
the face forces below the section sum to minus the section row.
- STOP_0, -X lug, sideways (from r1g6_stop0.lug_sideways_split.txt):
  - The section's Fx is +25.6 N: the hub above holds the lug's lower part inward. So our side's net push on the lug's lower part
    is about 26 N outward (-X).
  - The outer face (n = -X) can only push the lug inward. Its real compressive contact is +9.2 N inward (centre Y 293.2, Z 9.7;
    nodal peak 9.75 MPa). The face row's net -32 N comes from -41 N of tensile extrapolated nodal values, which a contact face
    cannot give. That -32 N is withdrawn.
  - The outward push sits at the edges of the LUMPED plate boss: the front Z face's 1 mm band along the boss outline carries
    Fx -19.9 N, and the back face's -2.4 N. The flat interior and the hole rim carry about 0 N in X, as a frictionless face should.
    The lumped boss's step edge acts as a shoulder on the lug. A real, separate plate has no step and cannot do this.
  - In the real joint, that outward push of about 22-26 N goes through friction under the bolt clamp at the plates (0.3 N.m: about
    0.33-0.5 kN preload, so about 200-300 N of friction capacity). Failing that, it goes onto the bolt: the -X shank's gap on that
    side is already down to 0.102 here. It does NOT go onto the outer face, which can only push inward.
  - For the shoulder thread's stop case:
    - the lug's outer face takes at most about 9 N inward (+X) from our pocket wall, near Y 293.2, Z 9.7;
    - the lug's net sideways load is about 26 N outward (-X), to be applied at the lug's Z faces (friction) or at its bolt hole
      (bearing), not at the outer face.
- The face rows below are the raw integrals. Read the outer-face and Z-face Fx through the lines above.
