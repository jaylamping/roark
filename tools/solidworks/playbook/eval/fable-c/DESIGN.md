# The organic forearm link: design note

**The idea.** A bone: the flat elbow disc swells into an oval shaft, the shaft waists slightly and spreads into two
condyles that are the fork cheeks, as one skin whose section changes all the way and never stops.

**How it is made.** One loft through 13 superellipse sections (lens at the disc, ellipse in the shaft, rounded
rectangle at the fork), its last section turned about the pin axis for the cheek ends; an arched slot; a bullnosed rim.
The cable runs straight from the crotch up the shaft to a slit in the back. 136.5 g against 261.1; lowest factor 2.58.

**Tried and dropped.**
- *Idea B, the wishbone* (`pictures/idea_b.png`): two blades that part from the disc, open between. It reads as a
  bracket or tongs, not a limb, and the cable would hang in the open V.
- *Cable mouth in the neck (round 1):* the neatest mouth, but it cut the thickest fibres where +Y bends the link
  hardest: shear between layers 1.42 printed flat, yield 2.07. Moved down the shaft, neck deepened.
- *Sections as single closed splines:* the loft twisted and pinched to a point. Two half curves per section cured it.
- *Printing flat on its back* (far less support): fails shear between layers, 1.42, at the step on the disc face.

**With another hour.** (1) A real blend, R3 to R4, where the shaft comes out of the disc: it is the place that still
looks assembled and the stress raiser that forbids the flat print. (2) Spend some of the 125 g left on depth in Y:
the pin moves 5.4 mm under 60 N in +Y, against 0.3 mm by hand for the boxy beam. (3) More life in the shaft, which is
still close to a straight handle between Z -60 and -150.

**Least happy with.** The junction of neck and disc. From the front the outline has a notch where the neck meets the
rim; from the side there is a step where the swell lifts off the flat disc face. The bone grows out of the shaft into
the fork, but at the elbow it is still attached to a washer with a rounded edge.
