# C2 restatement (written before any modelling), run sonnet-c2

C1 finished 09:36 (start of the 100 min was 09:35). C2 started 09:37.

1. **Purpose.** A printed PETG forearm link: bolts to a motor output at the elbow end (six M3 on a 50 mm circle, 30 mm bore), carries a 60 N pin load at the wrist (+X, -X, +Y) back into the elbow face, passes a 6 mm cable straight from the elbow to the wrist, and has a fork at the wrist for a part that swings about the pin.
2. **Fixed (measured on the finished solid).** Flat ring on Y -4 between D30 and D62; six D3.4 holes on D50 (first on +X, 60 degrees apart); D30 bore; a D7 clear cylinder 10 mm long on the +Y side in front of each hole (Y 4 to 14); pin D8 at X 0, Z -200 along Y; cheek inner faces at Y +-16 flat over at least D16 round the pin; at least 5 mm of material round the pin hole; clear slot between the cheeks from Z -183 downward (and so the D30 swing cylinder); a straight clear D6 cable passage from elbow end to wrist end that stays inside the form.
3. **Good, the look.** One continuous grown form: round and oval sections that change slowly, ends that swell out of the middle, no flat plates or sharp corners except where the task makes a face flat (mounting ring, cheek inner faces).
4. **Good, the numbers.** Mass at or under the boxy link (261.0 g, 219,436 mm3). Factor at least 2.0 on von Mises (45 MPa), on tension across layers (22.5 MPa) and on shear between layers (13 MPa), for 60 N at the pin in +X, -X, +Y with the elbow ring held, in the print direction I choose. One piece, no enclosed cavity, fits a 256 mm bed.
5. **Mine to choose.** Section shapes, taper, how the ends grow out of the middle, where material is and is not, the print direction, the outer size of the elbow flange beyond D62, the route of the cable passage.

**What "organic" means here, in my words:** the link reads as one tapering bone with a swollen head at each end, where every surface is curved and flows into the next with generous radii, and the only flat faces are the ones a screw head or a swinging part sits on.

**Three things I will not do.**
- Build it from boxes and cylinders with the joints left as steps (no plates, no ribs stuck on, nothing that sticks out of the silhouette).
- Hollow it into an enclosed cavity, or leave a cable lying outside the form.
- Move or shrink a fixed item (ring, holes, bore, screw clearance, pin, cheek faces, slot) to make the shape easier.
