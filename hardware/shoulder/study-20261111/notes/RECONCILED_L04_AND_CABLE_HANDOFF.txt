FULL-ASSEMBLY ROUTING -- WHAT TO DO NEXT
Map each cable span's endpoints and fixation to torso, P1/roll housing, P2/RS02 housing, yaw output/upper arm and downstream joints. Distinguish interfaces moving together from spans crossing a joint. Use the actual selected connector, latch, boot, cable construction/diameter, supplier dynamic bend/torsion limits, slack length and anchor locations. Specify proposed diagnostic pitch/roll/yaw ranges and their coupled constraints; none is yet an accepted operating range. Preserve P1.
Test the full assembly, not only local P2: each joint individually, combined poses, intermediates, approach from both directions, reversals and service/removal poses. Include actual screws, stops, housings, torso, arm members and covers relevant to the route. Check complete connector withdrawal plus latch/finger/tool access, not only straight nominal shaft cylinders.
Use continuous sweeps or conservative bounded interpolation/refinement where justified. Finite sampled zero-collision results do not alone prove clearance between poses. A rigid tube/plug sweep can reveal interference, but does not predict actual cable shape, slack migration, snap-through, torsion, connector force, abrasion or fatigue. Account for relative structural deflection and manufacturing/assembly allowances;1mmnominal gap is not a completed allowance budget.
Track possible kinks/tight curvature, taut spans, slack loops entering pinch regions, tangles at reversals, rubbing on edges/bolt heads/stops, and motor heat. Check retention on actual structural anchors with removable service access. Extra ties can move/concentrate the bend; do not transmit flex directly into the connector or assume more restraint is always better. Protective liners/guides must stay retained and consume real space; they are not substitutes for a viable route or sound roof/seat geometry.

ESSENTIAL PHYSICAL VALIDATION -- LATER AUTHORIZED PLAN, NOT ACTION NOW
Use the real cable/connector and an externally supported, de-energized representative mockup after assembly/structure safety is established. Verify seated/key/latch/withdrawal operation, then gently traverse only a pre-established safe diagnostic envelope, separately and in combinations, from both directions. Mark cable/anchor positions, record loop migration, tightest curvature, twist, connector displacement and repeatability. Stop before binding, tautness, contact damage or forceful joint movement; do not use the cable to discover an unknown hard limit.
Inspect jacket for rubbing/scuffs/fraying/whitening, kinks/flattening, support-scar contact, anchor slip and connector movement. Distinguish normal intended sliding against a designed guide from damaging abrasion. Repeat service removal/reinstallation. Define cyclic testing from actual cable specifications, intended duty and measured routing behavior; no invented cycle count, bend radius, torque or proof-load threshold. A brief hand-cycle check is an early defect screen, not fatigue-life validation. Electrical continuity checks need a separately defined safe unpowered procedure and do not alone prove signal integrity or fatigue resistance.
